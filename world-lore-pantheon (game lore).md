# Guild-Idler — World Truths & Pantheon

A reference doc for future narrative expansion, not in-game text. Two
capstone-tier chains have established two distinct kinds of divinity in this
setting; this doc pins down the rules so future chains stay consistent with
them, and lists what's been established vs. what's still open to invent.

---

## Guilds — what they are, and what they can become

Guilds have always existed in some form. There's no founding myth and no
single "first guild" — the institution predates recorded history the same
way roads and marketplaces do. At their most common, a guild is exactly
what it looks like from the outside: a free-lance collective. Someone with
a problem posts it, or asks around, and the guild filters the work out to
whoever's available and suited to it — odd jobs, escort work, monster
clearing, courier runs, whatever pays. Most guilds live and die at exactly
this scale. A few dozen members, a guildhall that's really just a room
above a tavern, in and out of the historical record within a generation.

Occasionally — rarely, and never on purpose — a guild stops being that.
Somewhere in its own history it stops taking whatever's posted on the board
and starts being asked directly instead of posting to one at all, and
eventually becomes the reason a town, a border, or a bastion is still
standing. It turns from a business into an institution: something people
orient their safety around, not a service they occasionally hire. Nobody
plans this. No founding charter states it as a goal. It happens because a
guild kept saying yes to the hardest job on the board for long enough that
eventually the hardest job was the only one left worth taking.

**This guild has already made that turn on-page.** `proving_the_bastion`
(reqLevel 16) is the exact hinge point — Captain Thorne doesn't hand over
IronRest Bastion as a contract fee, she hands it over because the guild
proved, repeatedly, that it was the kind of guild that does that. Every
chain from `granite_crossing` onward reads differently once this is named:
the guild isn't picking up freelance work at that point anymore, it's
*defending territory it now considers its own.* This isn't a retcon —
it was already the shape of the existing chain order — this section just
gives the pattern a name so future chains keep writing to it rather than
accidentally sliding the guild back to purely mercenary status.

### Guild relations: rivalry, with a floor

Guilds compete — for contracts, territory, reputation, the best recruits —
and that competition is real and constant, especially among ordinary
free-lance guilds all fishing from the same board. But it has a hard floor.
When something is bad enough, guilds fight alongside each other, no formal
alliance required, rivalry or not. This is truest of guilds that have made
the turn described above — an ascended guild recognizes what another one
has become even through the rivalry, and the instinct to answer when
something is genuinely bad runs deeper than any territorial dispute.

**There is no separate title for an ascended guild.** The difference isn't
a name, a rank, or a formal classification — it's reach. The world simply
knows some guilds by name, in more places, for bigger reasons, than others.
A three-person free-lance outfit and a founding guild that now guards a
whole border region are structurally the same kind of thing. One is just
known.

**Open question for future worldbuilding:** are there other guilds
elsewhere that have made this same transition — rivals, allies, or ones
that tried and failed? The wrecked banners in `last_god`'s Field of Failed
Guilds are one obvious place a failed one could already be hiding, unnamed,
if a future writer wants to plant it — and per the above, a failed rival
guild's banner sitting there is exactly the kind of loss the guild's own
allies-despite-rivalry instinct would take personally.

## Geography

Kept intentionally sparse — three loose bands, not a full atlas. Enough
structure to place a new chain without contradicting the next one, no more:

1. **The Kingdom (unnamed so far).** The settled heartland, where ordinary
   life and the guild's own earliest contracts take place — the mill in
   `millers_problem`, Thornhollow, Greywick. Governed by an actual crown,
   never yet named on-page, though kingship itself is clearly a
   load-bearing idea in this setting (see below).
2. **The Reach — a frontier line of holdfasts.** Emberfell ("northernmost
   of the holdfasts," `dragon_hunt`), IronRest Bastion, Granite Crossing,
   and the farm at the edge all sit along this line — the boundary between
   the settled Kingdom and open, contested, monster-thick territory. This
   is where the guild's own transformation happens (`proving_the_bastion`
   onward) and where most of the demon/Harrower-thread chains are set.
3. **Beyond any map at all.** Where `world_ender` and `last_god` happen —
   not a place with a name or a direction, reached by a road that
   shouldn't exist or a sea heading that's been sailed before but never
   charted. Deliberately outside the Kingdom/Reach structure entirely.

**Worth noticing, not meant to be over-explained:** several unrelated
factions and entities style themselves with the language of kingship — the
goblins have a king (`goblin_warband`), the demons an "Unmade King"
(`world_ender`), the undead menace is titled the Hollow *King* and gets a
*Second Coronation* (`hollow_king`), and two early chains revolve around
crowns (`ancient_crown`, `lost_kingdom`). Treat these as self-styled titles
— grandiosity, hubris, factions borrowing the trappings of authority for
themselves — rather than evidence of one connected mechanism or hidden
order behind them. Deliberately left loose so it stays flavor a reader can
enjoy noticing, not a thread a future writer feels obligated to pay off.

## Where monsters come from

Two categories, deliberately not one:

**Native.** Most of what the guild fights is simply from here — goblins,
dragons, whatever was under the miller's cellar, raiders, corrupt
captains, rival guild leaders. No special origin needed; this is the
default. Only reach for the second category when a chain specifically
wants otherworldly flavor.

**Otherworldly (demons, and worse).** These arrive through old wounds in
the world, not one single invasion. At some point in the past — sometimes
on purpose, sometimes by accident — mages tried to open a connection to
somewhere else: communication, divination, summoning, it varies. Some of
those attempts worked, badly, and left a scar behind instead of a clean
door. Most of those scars are old, faint, and dormant now. Occasionally,
for reasons nobody fully controls, one opens just wide enough for
something on the other side to physically step through for a short time
before it closes again. This is why demon incursions in this setting read
as sudden and localized (`crows_warning`, `harrowers_foot`) rather than one
world-ending invasion event — they're leaks, not a war front. Not yet.

**Confirmed:** the Harrower uses these existing old wounds to extend its
own influence — every demon proxy in the existing chain thread (foot
soldiers, the general, the risen paladin) is coming through leaks that were
already there, not something the Harrower tore open itself. But those
leaks are sized for what they've always let through: small, brief, mortal
or near-mortal things. They aren't remotely large enough for the Harrower
itself. Its own crossing needs something built on purpose, at a scale no
accidental scar has ever reached — which is exactly what the ritual
components (the altar, and whatever else joins it) are actually for. The
Harrower isn't hunting for a bigger *existing* wound to widen. It's
building a door.

## Two kinds of divinity

### Old Gods
Never needed worship, and don't care whether they get it. They existed
before belief was invented as a concept, and "god" is just the closest word
mortals have for something that size and that hungry — not a title it
claimed for itself. Full of hubris and hunger rather than doctrine or
domain. They don't consume in service of anything; they consume because
that's what they are.

**They have physical forms.** Not abstractions, not pure concept — actual
bodies, actual beings, in the Cthun / Yog-Saron sense rather than a purely
metaphysical force. The range runs from barely distinguishable from human
(the Severer) to something that barely reads as a coherent shape at all
(the World-Ender). All four are meant to eventually be beatable in a fair
fight, given the right approach — see "How Old Gods fall," below.

Mechanically and narratively:
- **No fixed name.** Every culture that's encountered one calls it something
  different, and none of the names are "correct" — this is a deliberate
  setting rule, not inconsistent writing. Don't ever give an Old God a
  canonical name — the titles below (Harrower, World-Ender, Kindler,
  Severer) are writer's-reference shorthand, not in-fiction proper nouns
  every culture would agree on.
- **No fixed location, necessarily.** The World-Ender doesn't have an
  address — the road, hills, and sky rearrange to meet whoever gets close
  enough to matter. Not every Old God needs this exact trait — some may
  already have a limited, ongoing presence in the mortal plane rather than
  being fully absent until summoned.
- **Established/planned examples:** The World-Ender (`world_ender`, reqLevel
  34, defeated), the Harrower (planned, in progress toward a crossing — see
  its own section below), the Kindler (planned, War), and the Severer
  (planned, Strife).

### What Old Gods want (design framework: "The Four")

Not in-world lore — nobody in the setting groups these together, and per
the "no fixed name" rule above, no scholar-character should ever say
"Pestilence" or "Death" out loud as a proper title. This is a **writer's
tool**, styled loosely after the Four Horsemen, to keep each Old God's
appetite distinct from the others rather than having every one of them
default to generic "ancient evil wants to eat the world."

| Domain | Old God | Physical Form | Wants | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Death / Undeath** | **The Harrower** | Grotesque, humanoid-adjacent — a mass built from the dead it's already claimed, closer to a body than not | Life essence, souls — consumed directly, in person | Cannot currently cross over. Gathers the ritual components (see altar premise below) needed to open a physical crossing into the mortal plane, using demon proxies to search while it waits. |
| **Pestilence / Decay** | **The World-Ender** | Barely a coherent shape at all — "the size of a held breath," the far non-humanoid extreme | The world itself, consumed slowly, from the edges inward | Doesn't need to *cross* over — already present in some diminished, contained sense. Something (the Vigil, tended by exhausted unnamed things) has been holding the actual rot back since before anyone kept records, and that containment is failing, not being besieged. |
| **War** | **The Kindler** | A colossal, upright mass fused from armor, banners, and weapons out of every war it's ever touched — no single face; soldiers' faces surface and submerge across it like something boiling | Conflict itself — feeds on violence happening, not on who wins | Doesn't fight and doesn't need to march. Likely already has some limited, roving presence near major mortal conflicts rather than being fully absent — a strong tie-in to `granite_crossing`'s guild rivalry or `proving_the_bastion`'s border war, if a future writer wants to reveal one was seeded rather than organic. |
| **Strife** | **The Severer** | The most humanoid of the four by a wide margin — passes convincingly as a person; its rare glimpsed "true form" is barely different from human at all, which is worse | Broken trust, betrayal — not bloodshed, the breakdown that makes bloodshed easy | Already embedded, likely — trusted, charming, whoever a given room needs it to be, until whatever bond it's cultivating breaks at the worst possible moment. Best fit for a chain with no combat capstone at all; a social/political unraveling instead. |

Only the World-Ender and Harrower rows are load-bearing (one defeated, one
mid-arc). The Kindler and Severer are now named and given a physical form
and a want, but their actual confrontation chains are still fully open —
titles and shape only, deliberately, so a future writer isn't boxed in.

### How Old Gods fall

All four are meant to eventually be beatable — this isn't cosmic horror
where the answer is always "you can't win," it's cosmic horror where the
horror is in how much it costs to win. Two confirmed defeat vectors,
and either is valid for any of the four depending on what a given chain
needs:

1. **It crosses over, and is felled here.** The default so far. The
   World-Ender's crossing is passive (the world rearranges around it); the
   Harrower's planned arc is active (it's working toward a crossing on
   purpose). Either way, the fight happens on the mortal plane, on terms
   the guild has some home-field advantage in.
2. **The guild reaches its home plane instead.** Rather than waiting for a
   crossing, the guild finds — or is given — a way through: an artifact, a
   ritual, help from something like the Watchers, or wreckage left by a
   prior failed expedition (see the Field of Failed Guilds pattern from
   `last_god` — that's a reusable structural beat, not exclusive to True
   Gods). This is higher-risk, higher-reward narratively: no home-field
   advantage, but it means the guild chooses the timing instead of reacting
   to it.

The Kindler and the Severer, given their described forms, lean toward
option 2 by default — both already sound like they have *some* standing
presence in or near the mortal plane rather than being fully sealed away,
which makes "wait for it to try to cross" a weaker hook for them than it is
for the Harrower. Not a hard rule, just the natural read of what's written
above.

### True Gods
Real deities in the traditional sense: born of or sustained by belief,
worship, ritual, and purpose. They have names, domains, alignments, and
history. They can be good, evil, or anything between — and, critically,
**they can die.** Not always violently — often by attrition, outliving their
worshippers until there's nothing left to be a god of.

**Established example:** The Last God (`last_god`, reqLevel 55) — a
psychopomp, the god of the last mile. Sought out in person, in the final
days of life, to be walked personally into whatever comes after death.
Starved of belief over a very long time until it was barely capable of
answering its own altar. The guild's final act in `last_god` isn't a fight —
it's finishing the one service the god used to provide, for the god itself,
since nobody else was left to do it.

## Fates a True God can meet (taxonomy for future chains)

Use this when inventing a new god-tier antagonist or ally. Three states,
deliberately distinct so future chains don't retread `last_god`:

1. **Active** — worshipped, functioning, entirely off-screen so far. Assume
   most "normal" divine references already in the game (the Ashen Hand's
   paladins, Saint Aurlias, any future oath or shrine) point to gods of this
   kind unless a chain says otherwise. Good source for a future *ally* deity
   or a quest given directly by a god's clergy.
2. **Starved** — outlived its faith, faded rather than turned. Doesn't get
   cruel, just gets less and less able to do the thing it was a god of.
   **The Last God is this.** A second Starved God would need a different
   domain and a different ending beat to avoid repeating the "mercy-kill"
   structure — consider having the guild *save* one instead, for contrast.
3. **Turned** — the dark path: a True God that outlives everything so
   thoroughly it stops resembling what it was and starts resembling an Old
   God instead — hunger without the doctrine left to temper it. **Unused so
   far.** Strong twist-capstone material: set a chain up reading exactly
   like a Starved-God mercy-kill, then reveal it's Turned instead and the
   "kindness" was a trap.

## The Watchers

First encountered in `world_ender` guarding the World-Ender, but explicitly
**not in its service** — "the arrangement runs the other way." An order
older than the World-Ender itself, keeping count of something across
multiple fronts that have nothing to do with it. What they're counting, and
toward what threshold, is deliberately unestablished.

**Use for:** gating or foreshadowing future Old-God-tier content without
inventing a new mechanism every time. A Watcher whose count reaches zero
somewhere else in the world is a ready-made hook for a future capstone —
the guild wouldn't be discovering a new threat from scratch, just discovering
that one of the Watchers' other tallies just ran out.

The Watchers line in `world_ender` ("this is not the only tally they keep")
is a plant for future Old-God content generally — it doesn't have to be the
Harrower specifically, and doesn't need resolving until a chain actually
needs it. Treat the Harrower (below) as a separate, already-active thread
rather than the payoff to this one, unless a future writer decides to
connect them on purpose.

## Quick reference: what's confirmed vs. open

| Entity | Type | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The World-Ender | Old God — Pestilence | Defeated (`world_ender`) | No name, no fixed location, ends things because it can |
| The Last God | True God — Starved | Defeated / mercy-ended (`last_god`) | Psychopomp; god of the last mile; starved of belief, not corrupted |
| The Watchers | Neither — older order | Alive, unresolved | Keep multiple tallies; not servants of any Old God; source of future hooks |
| The Harrower | Old God — Death (planned) | Alive, at large, unconfronted | See dedicated section below — do not fight directly until its own chain exists |
| The Kindler | Old God — War (planned) | Alive, at large, unconfronted | Titled and given a form only; no chain material written yet |
| The Severer | Old God — Strife (planned) | Alive, at large, unconfronted | Titled and given a form only; no chain material written yet |
| An Active God | True God — Active | **Unwritten** | Good source for a future ally/patron deity |
| A Turned God | True God — Turned | **Unwritten** | Best used as a twist capstone — dress it as a Starved-God chain first |

## The Harrower (planned Old God — not yet confronted)

Already present by name across five existing chains (`crows_warning`,
`harrowers_foot`, `demon_generals_ledger`, `what_the_culled_become`,
`the_pale_rider`), spanning reqLevel 8 through 32 — but never once appears
in person, and its actual goal was left deliberately unanswered
(`harrowers_foot`: "whatever is directing these raids wants something
specific, and it is getting closer to finding it"). This is the answer to
that open question, reserved for a future story pack rather than retconning
anything already written.

**Nature:** An Old God — no fixed name of its own (mortals call it "the
Harrower" only because that's what the demon general's ledger happened to
call it), full domain over undeath and the raising of the dead. What it
wants is simple underneath all of it: life essence, souls, taken directly
and in person — not through proxies, which is exactly what it's been
limited to so far. Unlike the World-Ender, it isn't indifferent — it wants
a specific outcome: a physical crossing into this plane, so it can finally
feed here itself instead of directing others to do the finding for it. It
currently cannot cross. Its demon proxies reach the mortal plane through
old, pre-existing wounds in reality (see "Where monsters come from,"
above) — leaks that were already there and were never its own doing. Those
leaks are far too small for the Harrower itself, though, which is why it
has spent the length of the existing Harrower-thread chains directing its
proxies to search for the components needed to build something much
larger: a door sized for a god, not a crack sized for a demon.

**Why this fits everything already written:** every undeath beat across the
existing chains — the bite-curse in `crows_warning`, the graves opening
from the inside in `what_the_culled_become`, the Pale Rider being a risen
paladin rather than a living one — was already its domain bleeding through
its proxies, not incidental horror flavour. It never shows up in person in
any of those chains because it structurally *can't* yet.

**Seed premise for the eventual confrontation chain(s):** a town is razed
the same way as all the others — searched, not looted — but this time, at
the center of it, a dig-out has already started. What the guild eventually
finds, mid-excavation, is an altar: one of the components the Harrower
needs to open a crossing, not the only one. This is the point where "the
Harrower wants something specific" stops being a rumor and starts being a
race — every future chain in this thread should escalate toward the
Harrower actually finding what it needs, not away from it.

**Open design questions for whoever writes this pack:**
- How many components total, and does the guild ever get all of them first —
  or does the whole point become fighting the Harrower *after* it's already
  partially crossed over, which would be a very different (and very strong)
  capstone shape than either `world_ender` or `last_god`.
- Level placement: the existing thread already runs 8–32. A full
  confrontation arc should sit above that, likely alongside or beyond the
  existing 34/45/55 capstone band rather than folding into it.
- Whether the Watchers' "not the only tally" line (`world_ender`) is ever
  meant to connect to this — i.e., is the Harrower one of the fronts they're
  counting on? Not required, but available if useful.
