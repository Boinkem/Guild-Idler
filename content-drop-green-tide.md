# Content Drop: The Green Tide (Level 55 → 65)

Companion to `world-lore-pantheon.md` and `guild-idler-status.md`, scoped to
this one content drop. Holds the full chain-by-chain story, dungeon/raid
specs, and gear list for the arc so `guild-idler-status.md` doesn't have to
carry story-bible detail. Status doc gets a summary + link once this is
locked; this file is the source of truth for the actual content.

**Storytelling rule for this drop (and going forward):** be direct. Tell the
player what's happening as it happens — the god, the conduits, the motive —
rather than leaving it implicit or "mystical." Confirmed course-correction
from earlier chains, which erred too far toward ambiguity.

---

## Structure

Six beats, strictly linear (`requiresChainId` gating throughout, no
branching), one every ~2 levels from 55 to 65:

| Level | Beat | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 55 | Smoke on the Ridge | Quest chain |
| 57 | The Warlord's Banner | Quest chain |
| 59 | The Hungering Totem | Quest chain → unlocks Dungeon: The Warpriest's Rite |
| 61 | An Unlikely Alliance | Quest chain → unlocks Dungeon: The Strategist's Gambit |
| 63 | Tide Unbound | Quest chain |
| 65 | Siege of Skarnhold | Raid (~5 stages) |

**Antagonist: Grennoch.** Minor-domain True God, **Turned** — the first
Turned god this setting has ever put on-screen (per the pantheon's existing
taxonomy, previously unwritten). Originally a real, worshipped god of wild,
untamed growth. Rather than fading slowly the way a Starved god does,
Grennoch went dormant — a long slumber, cause unstated/deliberately
unexplained — and woke hungry. It found goblins and orcs already there:
both Chaotic Evil by nature, both ready and willing to embrace the power on
offer with zero reluctance. **Nobody is corrupted against their will in
this arc** — Grennoch offers, evil things accept, and everyone gets what
they wanted. **This is not a confused or sympathetic figure and should
never read as one** — it knows exactly what it's doing, has done it before
(see Chain 3), and the goblins/orcs are simply the latest willing fuel.

**Control vs. manipulation, by species (confirmed):**
- **Goblins — controlled.** Weaker-willed, primal, faith-driven. Grennoch
  dominates them directly. The Warpriest is a true conduit/instrument —
  devoted, not really steering anything of his own.
- **Orcs — manipulated, not controlled.** Smart enough to take the power
  and use it for their own ends. The Strategist is bargaining with
  Grennoch, not possessed by it — same as Grimtusk himself.

**What it actually feeds on:** chaos and discord broadly — warring,
fighting, infighting — not specifically the act of war itself. Leaves room
for future beats built on internal treachery/disorder within the warbands
themselves, not just battlefield combat.

No mercy-kill framing, no "tragic god" beat anywhere in this arc. Player
never fights Grennoch directly this arc — the fight is against what it's
choosing to empower in others. Epithet used once the guild identifies it
properly (Chain 3 onward): "the Green Mother" — used with dread, not pity.
Title payoff: "The Green Tide" is what Grennoch does, every time it gets
the chance.

**Grimtusk — resolved.** Fully Evil by nature (genre-standard orc
archetype, no ambiguity needed), not manipulated or unwilling — he's
handed power to dominate and takes it with open eyes, using Grennoch the
same way Grennoch uses him. No separate tragic angle for him either; the
arc has zero sympathetic figures on the antagonist side by design.

**Gear:**
- **Tuskbound** (dungeon set) — reqLevel ~59–61. Split loot: each dungeon
  drops a fixed, non-overlapping subset; both required for the full set.
- **Warhide** (raid set) — reqLevel 65, true BiS for this cap.

**New system this drop introduces:** Dungeons — generic, reusable,
2-hero, Normal/Heroic/Mythic tiers (same shape as raids). Built once,
available to every future content drop.

## Chain 1: Smoke on the Ridge (Level 55)

**Premise:** Goblin raids along the Reach escalate from opportunistic to
coordinated. Something is directing them that wasn't there before.

**Beats (draft):**
1. **Cold open, a burned holdfast.** The guild arrives at a Reach farmstead
   post-raid — not the usual goblin mess. Grain stores emptied
   systematically, not looted at random. Livestock driven off, not
   slaughtered on the spot. A survivor says the goblins moved "like they'd
   done it before," in silence, on a signal the survivor never saw given.
2. **Tracking the warband.** Standard combat step, but the guild finds
   something concrete along the trail: crude standards/totems the goblins
   are now carrying into raids, unlike anything seen from this species
   before. Player-facing text describes them plainly — carved, painted,
   *new* — not "strange" or "unsettling" without saying why.
3. **A second raid, larger.** The warband hits a second holdfast while the
   guild is still investigating the first — confirms this isn't a one-off,
   it's a pattern accelerating in real time.
4. **Chain close.** The guild identifies a single warband banner repeating
   across both sites — not "a goblin problem," *one* goblin force, growing
   fast and moving with a coordination goblins don't have on their own.
   Direct, stated hook into Chain 2: they're being led by someone new, and
   whoever it is needs finding before a third holdfast falls.

**Holdfast name: Bramblewatch** — a minor Reach watch-post, invented for
this arc rather than reusing an established location. Deliberately
thorn/overgrowth-flavored in the name itself, a quiet early resonance with
Grennoch's domain that reads as pure coincidence on a first playthrough.

**Orc seeding (Chain 1):** one small, plain physical detail in beat 2 —
among the tracks leaving Bramblewatch, a second set that isn't goblin-sized
or -shaped. Not commented on by any character, not flagged as mysterious —
just there for an attentive player to notice, with the reveal proper
landing in Chain 2.

## Chain 2: Grimtusk's Banner (Level 57)

**Premise:** The guild runs the repeating banner from Chain 1 back to its
source and finds a war-camp too organized to be goblin-led alone — and an
ally nobody expected.

**Beats (draft):**
1. **Following the banner.** Standard tracking/combat step, ends at a
   staging camp far larger than a single warband should be able to field —
   multiple bands' worth of goblins, one banner over all of them.
2. **The reveal, stated plainly.** Scouting the camp's edge, the guild
   sees them directly: orcs, fighting and camped alongside the goblins,
   not raiding them or being enslaved by them — allied. Text says exactly
   this, no hedging ("orcs and goblins, fighting side by side" rather than
   "shapes moving in the firelight"). A goblin/orc alliance is stated as
   unheard of on its own merits — neither side needing the other, historically
   at odds when they cross paths at all.
3. **Identifying Grimtusk.** The one banner unifying every warband
   belongs to an orc — Grimtusk — not a goblin. The coordination goblins
   couldn't generate on their own is explained the moment the player
   learns who's actually leading: an outsider imposing structure a
   scattered species never had reason to build for itself. Grimtusk reads
   as a genuinely competent unifier here, not (yet) supernatural — the
   scary part is that he's smart, not that he's touched by anything.
4. **A holdfast falls.** While the guild closes in on the camp, word
   arrives that **IronRest Bastion** has actually fallen — not raided,
   held. First time this arc a fixed, named location changes hands rather
   than just getting hit and abandoned.
5. **Chain close.** In the aftermath of retaking or confirming IronRest's
   fall, the guild notices something physically wrong with some of the
   captured/fallen fighters — glassy, unfocused eyes, wounds they should
   have gone down from, no sign of exhaustion after what must have been
   a full day's fighting. Described concretely, not as vague dread. Direct
   hook into Chain 3: something is being *done to* these fighters, and the
   guild needs to find out what — and whether Grimtusk knows it too.

## Chain 3: The Hungering Totem (Level 59)

**Premise:** The guild traces the wrongness in captured/fallen fighters
back to its source — and finds out this has happened before.

**Beats (draft):**
1. **Investigating the afflicted.** Following up on Chain 2's close, the
   guild examines fighters showing the same signs — glassy eyes, no
   exhaustion, wounds that should have ended the fight. A totem carried by
   one of them is the physical link, matching the standards seen in
   Chain 1.
2. **The old battlefield.** Tracking the totem's origin leads to a ruin or
   long-dead battlefield, currently written off by history as an ordinary
   war between two peoples generations back. On-site evidence — the same
   totem carvings, the same wrongness in old remains — makes it plain this
   isn't Grennoch's first "green tide." Stated directly: this has happened
   before, to someone else, and nobody recognized it for what it was.
3. **Naming it.** Through a scholar/lorekeeper NPC or on-site record, the
   guild identifies Grennoch by name and nature — a Turned god, once
   worshipped for growth, now feeding on chaos on purpose. No hedging on
   intent; the text states plainly that this is deliberate, not confusion
   or accident.
4. **The Warpriest.** The guild identifies the living conduit currently
   channeling Grennoch into the warbands — a goblin Warpriest, performing
   rites at the heart of the war-camp, personally responsible for how
   organized and tireless the fighters have become.
   → unlocks **Dungeon: The Warpriest's Rite**
5. **Chain close.** With the conduit identified, the guild moves to strike
   it down before the totem network grows any further.

## Chain 4: An Unlikely Alliance (Level 61)

**Premise:** The guild learns the orc side of this alliance isn't just
following Grimtusk's banner — they're making their own calculated bargain
with Grennoch, and one orc in particular is the reason the warbands fight
with real tactical discipline, not just totem-fueled ferocity.

**Beats (draft):**
1. **A pattern too clean for goblins.** Following Chain 3's close, the
   guild notices the warbands aren't just stronger, they're being
   out-maneuvered by — feints, staged retreats, coordinated pincer moves.
   Stated plainly as a different kind of threat than the Warpriest's rites:
   this is tactics, not ritual.
2. **Intercepted plans.** A field operation nets captured orc battle-plans
   or a captured scout, confirming a single orc — known only as the
   Strategist, title-only — is directing warband movements directly, and
   is in her own, separate contact with Grennoch. Text is explicit that
   this is a deliberate bargain on her part, not possession: she's using
   the power, not used by it.
3. **The Gambit.** The guild moves on the Strategist's command post and
   walks into a prepared ambush — she read their approach in advance. A
   real tactical setback (not a wipe, but a forced retreat/regroup), there
   specifically to establish she's a genuine threat in her own right, not
   a secondary target riding Grimtusk's coattails.
4. **Turning the tables.** Second approach, guild uses what it learned
   from the failed first attempt to counter her tactics on her own terms.
5. **Chain close.** The Strategist identified as the second conduit,
   dealing with Grennoch by choice for her own advancement — no
   ambiguity about her willingness. Guild moves to strike her down.
   → unlocks **Dungeon: The Strategist's Gambit**

## Chain 5: Tide Unbound (Level 63)

**Premise:** With both conduits dead, Grennoch's precise, controlled setup
collapses — and rather than the fighting easing off, it gets worse, wider,
and less organized all at once, right before Grimtusk makes his own move.

**Beats (draft):**
1. **The fighting spreads.** Concretely worse, not vaguer: attacks now
   hitting places outside the warbands' usual range, afflicted fighters
   showing more extreme physical changes than anything seen in Chains 1-4.
2. **Infighting inside the warbands themselves.** A direct beat using the
   "feeds on chaos broadly, not just war" detail from Grennoch's profile —
   the guild witnesses or hears reports of goblins and orcs turning on each
   other, not just outward raiding. Stated plainly as Grennoch no longer
   caring which side the chaos comes from.
3. **The source, confirmed.** Scouts/a captured fighter/a lorekeeper
   confirms what's happening: Grimtusk himself has taken over as the
   direct channel, by his own choice, to finish his conquest outright. No
   hedging — he sought this deliberately, the same way the Strategist did.
4. **The mobilization.** Grimtusk pulls every remaining warband back to
   Skarnhold, consolidating for one final overwhelming stand/assault
   rather than continuing to spread thin — the guild's own cue that this
   is the endgame, not another skirmish.
5. **Chain close.** The guild rallies for the final march on Skarnhold.
   → unlocks **Raid: Siege of Skarnhold**

## Chain 6: Siege of Skarnhold (Level 65, Raid)

**Premise:** The guild storms Grimtusk's fortress-capital and ends both
threats at once — Grimtusk in combat, Grennoch by cutting the connection
that's sustaining it.

**Stages (draft, 6 encounters — every stage is a real fight, not a pure
narrative pause; the raid encounter model doesn't support non-combat
stages. All 6 carry **The Tide Presses** — see the Mechanic section
above):**
1. **The Outer Wall.** Mixed goblin/orc defenders — and the first sign of
   Chain 5's "chaos, not just war" detail made visible: pockets of
   defenders already fighting each other before the guild even reaches
   them, not in retreat, just turned.
2. **The War-Camp.** Through the gates now, clearing the camped-up army
   inside the walls — straightforward escalation, no lingering on what's
   no longer there.
3. **The Totem Grove.** Grennoch's influence at its visual peak —
   overgrowth, totems, the environment itself reading as "fed" for the
   first time in the arc. Guild fights through fodder to a paired
   mini-boss: a particularly juiced-up goblin and orc fighting together —
   a physical embodiment of the alliance itself, not just a solo
   lieutenant.
4. **The Elite Guard.** Grimtusk's personal honor guard at the throne
   approach — the tension-building stage, but still a genuine fight, not
   a cutscene.
5. **Grimtusk, Infused.** First phase — empowered, stronger, but still
   recognizably himself. The guild seems to win.
6. **Grimtusk, Transformed.** He doesn't go down — gives in further to
   the power in front of the party, into a grotesque, no-longer-quite-
   himself version for the true final fight. **On his death here, the
   connection severs and Grennoch drains back into slumber immediately**
   — visible in the environment itself (the grove's unnatural growth
   visibly wilting/receding as the fight ends), not just stated in a
   closing text block.

**Two fully separate, sequential encounters, not HP-based phases.** The
engine resolves per-encounter (success roll), not by tracking a boss's
health through phases the way a traditional RPG fight would — so
"Infused" and "Transformed" have to be two real, separate raid stages
(5 and 6), not one fight with a script-triggered form change partway
through. The flavor text carries the "he doesn't go down, he changes"
beat; the encounter model doesn't need to know anything special happened.

**Proposed `requiredRoles` (raid-wide, evaluated once — not per-stage,
matching how the existing raid system actually works):** melee 2,
ranged 2, caster 2 — matching Requiem's own capstone bar exactly, since
this is meant to be the same tier of "needs a real Heroic/Mythic roster"
challenge, not a step down from it. Open to pushing higher if Skarnhold
should read as a harder ask than Requiem specifically.

**Resolution note:** Grennoch is not destroyed, just drained — door left
open, not committed to, for a much later drop where it wakes again.

## Mechanic: The Tide Presses

**Green-Tide-wide, applies to all 6 Skarnhold raid stages and the
Warpriest's Rite boss encounter ("The Chant Deepens" is this mechanic's
in-fiction name for that specific fight).** Reuses the existing `speed`
mod and its already-established effect on quest/raid duration — no new
resolution system, no cross-stage state, no streak/momentum tracking.

- **Base penalty:** every affected encounter's baseline success chance is
  set 5% lower than an equivalent encounter would normally be tuned at —
  Grennoch's hunger makes this content harder by default, not just later
  if you're slow.
- **Speed offset:** each encounter independently reads the party's own
  speed-adjusted duration for that fight and grants back up to +5% success,
  scaled to how fast they are — a genuinely fast, speed-invested party can
  claw all the way back to parity with a normal encounter.
- **Deliberately capped at parity, never above it.** The offset can only
  cancel the base penalty, not exceed it — this is a real, permanent
  difficulty tax on this content, not a min-max opportunity to make it
  easier than normal raids/dungeons. A slow party stays meaningfully
  harder-off; a fast party just isn't punished for being slow.
- Evaluated fresh per encounter (same as every other per-encounter roll),
  so it sidesteps the cross-stage failure/reset problem entirely — no
  state carries between stages, each one just independently checks the
  same party-speed value.

**Totem consumables — direct counterplay, reusing the crafting system.**
Since speed is the exact lever The Tide Presses reads, a tiered line of
craftable speed consumables gives players a concrete way to push back
against the penalty without needing a bespoke new effect type:
- **Lesser Totem** — +1% speed
- **Minor Totem** — +2% speed
- **Standard Totem** — +3% speed

General-purpose speed consumables (not narrowly scoped to Skarnhold
specifically), but thematically tied to this drop and — proposed —
unlocked progressively alongside it: Lesser from clearing either dungeon,
Minor from clearing both, Standard from clearing the raid itself. Gives
players small, earned rewards throughout the arc rather than one single
endgame unlock, and ties the crafting payoff directly back into the one
new mechanic this drop introduces. **Open question:** confirm this
progressive unlock gating, or should all three be available from a single
unlock point instead?

**Design note:** both are 2-hero, Normal/Heroic/Mythic tiered like raids,
using the same `requiresChainId`/gating pattern raids already prove out.
Deliberately different tone from each other — the Warpriest's is
primal/ritual, the Strategist's is tactical/mechanical — matching the
control-vs-manipulation split already established for their species.

### The Warpriest's Rite (unlocked by Chain 3, ~Level 59-61)

Primal, ritual-site atmosphere — totems, chanting, blood-and-bone
trophies. **3 encounters:**
1. **Ritual Guards** — goblin zealots defending the approach, first close
   look at the totems seen in Chains 1/3 in physical form.
2. **The Chant Deepens** — goblins mid-rite feeding the totem. This is
   the in-fiction name for **The Tide Presses** (see the Mechanic section
   above): base success 5% lower than a normal encounter, offset up to
   parity by the party's own speed mods — a genuinely fast party isn't
   punished, a slow one feels the rite dragging on against them.
3. **The Warpriest** (boss) — fought mid-rite at the totem's heart; his
   magic punishes a party with no way to interrupt the ritual.

**Proposed requirement:** at least 1 caster of the 2 heroes brought.
**Drops:** Tuskbound Cowl, Tuskbound Fetish, Tuskbound Grips.

### The Strategist's Gambit (unlocked by Chain 4, ~Level 59-61)

Tactical command-post atmosphere — traps, ambush mechanics, disciplined
orc soldiers. **3 encounters, deliberately mirroring the Rite's structure
while playing differently:**
1. **The Outer Watch** — disciplined sentries spring a prepared ambush,
   direct callback to Chain 4's own "walked into a prepared ambush" beat.
2. **Killing Field** — positioning/hazard-driven, elevated defenders
   raining ranged fire.
3. **The Strategist** (boss) — punishes predictable, repeated tactics
   specifically; leaning on the same approach twice should visibly cost
   the party something.

**Proposed requirement:** at least 1 ranged of the 2 heroes brought.
**Drops:** Tuskbound Warblade, Tuskbound Hide, Tuskbound Treads.

**Open question:** exact per-tier (Normal/Heroic/Mythic) difficulty
scaling — deferred until the generic Dungeon system itself is scoped,
since that's shared plumbing for every future drop, not specific to
Green Tide. The single-role-per-dungeon requirement above is a proposal
for this drop specifically, not a confirmed system-wide pattern yet.

## Balance considerations

**Set-bonus power creep, deliberate not accidental.** Gear Score is
already `reqLevel`-scaled (patch 0157), so individual Warhide/Tuskbound
pieces should naturally outrank individual Requiem pieces of the same
rarity — that part's solved by existing systems. The real risk is set
*bonuses* specifically: if Warhide's bonus values are tuned at similar
magnitude to Requiem's rather than deliberately stronger, a player mid-
transition (partial Warhide, full Requiem) could rationally prefer
staying in — or actively re-farming — the old set, undermining the whole
point of the new gear chase. **Requirement for the eventual tuning pass:**
both Tuskbound and Warhide's set-bonus magnitudes need to scale up from
Requiem's, proportionally enough that even a partial new-set bonus is
competitive against Requiem's full one — not just a flat copy-paste at a
higher reqLevel. Exact values deferred to a real balance pass against
live `equipment.json`/set-bonus data, not decided here.

## Open design questions log

- God's nature: **Grennoch, Turned from the start** (confirmed) — no
  misdirection, no sympathetic framing at any point in the arc.
- Orc introduction — cold or lightly seeded (see Chain 1 note).
- Holdfast/location naming for Chain 1.
- Fortress name: **Skarnhold** (confirmed).
- Warboss name: **Grimtusk** (confirmed). One consistent character
  throughout the arc, not a separate mid-boss — introduced as the Warlord
  uniting goblins/orcs in Chain 2, and the same individual at the Chain 6
  raid, transformed by Chain 5's events (see below). Corrected from an
  earlier draft that mistakenly split him into two characters.
- **Grennoch's fate at Chain 6's close (confirmed):** not killed, drained.
  It poured everything into Grimtusk as its final vessel with nothing held
  in reserve — when he dies, that connection is severed completely, and
  Grennoch, fully overextended, collapses back into slumber on the spot.
  No separate binding rite/artifact needed. Leaves the door open for a
  much later, higher-level content drop where it wakes again, hungrier —
  not committed to, just left possible.
- Warpriest and Strategist: **title-only**, no personal names — keeps
  Grimtusk the arc's one named antagonist.
